Original content: Marketing: SEO tools marketers rely on (free & paid options)

Published on Wed, 29 Jul 2026 20:00:03 GMT

seo might feel like it’s under threat with the rise of AI. But the data tells a different story — SEO tools are still a must-have for marketers. In a HubSpot survey, 27% of marketers agreed that the biggest ROI channel of this year was the website, blog, and SEO. It’s fair to say that seo has changed, but it’s still an essential component of a successful marketing strategy. The key is having an excellent strategy, timely execution, and stakeholder buy-in, with reporting that proves the channel’s success. All of this hinges on picking the best SEO marketing software. It ‌means choosing a stack that drives the business forward without breaking the bank. Table of contents seo tools often have overlapping features, which makes it difficult to know which one (or ones) actually fit. The goal is a stack that covers all five of the below functions well, using as few tools as possible to keep costs down without sacrificing quality. Keyword research is the starting point for almost every seo initiative. It determines what content gets created and which SEO terms a page is optimized to rank for. seo tools help with keyword research by surfacing search volume, competition data, and related terms that would otherwise take hours to find manually. Keyword research tools support topic clustering and intent analysis, enabling marketers to group related terms and understand whether searchers are looking to learn, compare, or buy. This matters because targeting the wrong intent, even for a high-volume keyword, often leads to content that ranks but doesn’t convert. Technical seo covers the behind-the-scenes factors that determine whether search engines can crawl, index, and properly understand a site. SEO tools help with technical SEO audits by scanning for issues like broken links, duplicate content, slow load times, and indexing errors that would be nearly impossible to catch manually across a large site. Technical seo tools detect crawl issues, Core Web Vitals problems, and schema errors. These issues matter because a technically broken site limits how much keyword research and content work can pay off. Rank tracking shows where pages stand in search results over time, which makes it possible to connect seo work to actual outcomes rather than guesswork. SEO tools help with rank tracking by automatically monitoring keyword positions and flagging significant jumps or drops before they become bigger problems. Rank tracking tools monitor keyword positions across locations and SERP features. A single average ranking doesn’t tell the entire story when results vary by city or device, or when they appear within features like local packs and featured snippets. Featured Resource: How to Find SERP Feature Opportunities [+ Free tools] On-page optimization covers everything on a page itself, including titles, headers, body content, internal links, and image alt text. seo tools help with on-page content optimization by scoring content against ranking pages, suggesting related terms to include, and flagging missing elements like meta descriptions. On-page signals remain one of the clearest ways search engines determine relevance, and small fixes often produce outsized results compared to the effort involved. Reporting turns seo activity into evidence that the channel is working, which is often the difference between a program that keeps its budget and one that doesn’t. SEO tools help with SEO reporting by pulling traffic, ranking, and conversion data into dashboards that can be shared with stakeholders who don’t work in SEO daily. SEO results take time to materialize, and consistent reporting keeps leadership patient and engaged in the meantime. The tools below cover the five core jobs already outlined, organized by category so it’s easy to spot the right fit for each use case. HubSpot Website Grader is a free auditing tool that evaluates a website across four core categories. Marketers enter a URL and an email address, and within seconds, the tool returns an overall score out of 100, along with a breakdown for each of the four categories so it’s clear exactly where a site is strong and where it’s falling short. What sets Website Grader apart from a simple pass/fail checklist is that every issue it surfaces comes with a specific, actionable recommendation attached, rather than just a flag saying something is wrong. A marketer doesn’t need to know what a slow Largest contentful Paint (LCP) score means or how to fix the issue identified; the tool explains the issue and what to do about it in plain language. Because the report is structured this way, it works well as a recurring health check rather than a one-time scan. Running it periodically, especially after a site redesign, a CMS migration, or a wave of new content, makes it easier to catch regressions before they affect rankings. What I like: Website Grader makes it easy to see what’s going on with a website and how it scores across key components, then pairs each issue with a clear, specific recommendation on how to resolve it. That combination makes it especially useful for non-technical teams. Website Grader translates problems into next steps that a content marketer, a small business owner, or anyone without a development background can either act on or brief with confidence to a technical team. Google Search Console (GSC) is a free tool from Google that shows exactly how a website performs in Google Search, straight from the source. Since it pulls directly from Google’s own index and ranking data, it removes the guesswork that comes with third-party tools that estimate traffic and rankings rather than report them. What I like: Google Search Console is the best tool available for monitoring how a website actually performs in Google. It’s reporting real data rather than estimates. Third-party tools can only provide approximate rankings and traffic, but GSC shows exactly what’s happening, straight from the source. I like pairing GSC with the seo extension Search Analytics for Sheets. I use it to export keyword data and build keyword research directly from what a site is already ranking for. One approach I rely on is looking for high-impression keywords, since these usually represent terms where a site already has some authority and visibility, then checking the intent behind them. If the intent is transactional or consideration-stage, that’s a strong signal to build a content strategy around it. seo only earns continued investment when its results are visible, which is where reporting and SEO management tools come in. The platforms below cover dashboards, automated reporting, and CRM connection, the three pieces that turn SEO activity into evidence stakeholders can actually act on. HubSpot’s Marketing Hub brings seo reporting directly into the same platform where content, campaigns, and CRM data already live, removing one of the biggest friction points in proving SEO’s impact: the disconnect between traffic data and revenue data. Rather than pulling rankings from one tool, traffic from another, and pipeline data from a CRM that doesn’t talk to either, Marketing Hub connects organic performance to the contacts, deals, and revenue those visitors eventually generate. A blog post or landing page isn’t just judged by traffic or keyword position. It can be traced through to leads created, deals influenced, and closed revenue, all within the same dashboard. That connection between seo performance and CRM and revenue reporting is exactly what turns SEO from a cost center that’s difficult to defend into a channel with a clear, demonstrable return. Marketing Hub’s reporting tools allow custom dashboards built around the metrics that matter most to a specific business, whether that’s organic traffic by source or revenue attributed to organic content. seo teams can automate and schedule so stakeholders receive consistent updates. Because everything sits inside the same CRM-connected platform, it’s also possible to segment performance by lifecycle stage, see which organic content is driving the highest-value leads, and adjust strategy based on revenue rather than vanity metrics like page views alone. What I like: When a business is all-in on HubSpot, the reporting is second to none. Because the data links directly back to the CRM, it allows for the most impactful and sophisticated reporting available, since everything can be tied back to actual customers, sales, and revenue rather than stopping at traffic or rankings. That’s the difference between knowing a page is popular and knowing a page is profitable. Featured Resource: Learn more about seo and Reporting via this free HubSpot course.

Google Analytics (GA4) is Google’s free analytics platform. It gives a complete view of how visitors interact with a site across every channel, not just organic search.

Pricing: Free (Google Analytics 4)

What I like: GA4 is free and a must-have for any business. Analytics provide a layer of data that seo tools alone don’t capture. I most commonly use GA4 to review content performance holistically: a page might underperform by SEO metrics alone, but if it’s pulling in strong traffic according to Analytics, it’s still worth keeping rather than cutting. It’s easy for seo to work in a silo, judging success only by its own metrics, but GA4 provides the bigger picture. A second use case is pairing GA4 with Data Studio to build real-time reports, particularly around AI traffic performance. Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio) is Google’s free reporting tool, pulling data from multiple sources into a single, shareable dashboard that updates automatically. What I like: Clients get regularly updated, real-time reports without extra manual work. I let the data update automatically, then once a month I log in to add commentary explaining what the data means. The tool has streamlined reporting significantly, cutting out the repetitive work of rebuilding the same report from scratch every month. Keyword research tools vary widely in scope, from lightweight browser add-ons to full enterprise platforms covering every stage of an seo workflow. The two below sit at opposite ends of that range, making them useful for different budgets and team sizes. HubSpot’s seo tool, part of Marketing Hub, helps marketers create topic clusters. This approach is slightly different from keyword research. The tool encourages marketers to organize content into topics rather than treating each keyword as an isolated target. Each topic is built around a pillar page (a comprehensive resource covering a subject in depth) and supported by a network of subtopic content addressing narrower questions and long-tail variations within that subject. Rather than researching one keyword at a time and hoping the resulting pages eventually add up to topical authority, the tool is built around planning that authority from the outset. Inside the tool, marketers can add a core topic idea and see monthly search volume and difficulty data for potential subtopic keywords, which makes it easier to prioritize which subtopics are worth building content around before any writing begins. There’s room for up to 100 subtopic keywords per topic, giving plenty of space to map out a full cluster in advance rather than discovering gaps after the fact. Structuring content around topics has become more valuable as search has shifted toward rewarding topical depth over keyword density. Search engines and AI answer engines increasingly evaluate how thoroughly a site covers a subject, not just whether a single page targets the right keyword, and a well-built cluster signals that depth clearly. HubSpot’s State of AEO in 2026 found that pages featuring elements like outbound links, supporting statistics, and named author input earn more citations across AI Overviews, Gemini, and Perplexity. A pillar-and-cluster structure naturally creates room for that kind of supporting evidence across an entire topic rather than a single post. Because the tool sits inside the same platform as HubSpot’s broader content and reporting features, the keyword and difficulty data used to plan a cluster connects directly to the same dashboards used to measure how that content performs once it’s published. What I like: This tool makes it much easier to manage content strategy at scale, especially once a site has dozens or hundreds of articles published. Instead of tracking pillar and subtopic relationships in a separate spreadsheet, marketers can see the entire structure in one place and immediately spot cluster gaps or missing internal links. The tool also reinforces a shift I think more marketers need to make: thinking in topics rather than individual keywords. A single keyword target only gets a site so far, but a well-mapped topic shows search engines and AI answer engines that a site covers a subject in real depth. This matters more than ever for AEO, since answer engines tend to pull from sources that demonstrate consistent authority across a whole subject, not just one well-optimized page. Semrush is an enterprise-grade seo platform built to handle far more than keyword research, with AI-powered features layered across its toolset to speed up everything from topic discovery to content briefs. On the keyword side, it surfaces search volume, keyword difficulty, related terms, and intent data, while also identifying content gaps against competitors and suggesting topic clusters worth targeting. Its AI capabilities include features like automated content outlines and AI-assisted keyword grouping, which cut down on the manual work of sorting large keyword lists into usable themes.

Pricing: Paid, with plans starting around $139.95/month

What I like: Semrush is one of my favorite tools. I’ve always gravitated toward it, both because of the UX and because it covers the full breadth of seo needs, from keyword research through to backlink analysis, site auditing, and more, all without switching platforms.

Keywords Everywhere is a low-cost browser extension that overlays keyword data, including search volume, related terms, and trend information, directly onto search engine results pages and other sites where research naturally happens.

Pricing: Packages start from $7/month

What I like: Keywords Everywhere is so affordable that it’s worth considering for almost anyone. I’ve always paid for Semrush, but for a stretch of time I also kept up a Keywords Everywhere subscription, simply because it’s so cheap and it pulls keyword data like related terms and trends directly into the search results page, exactly where the research is already happening. It saves opening another tab or tool just to check a number. On-page optimization tools help close the gap between writing content and writing content that’s actually structured to rank, scoring drafts against what’s already performing and flagging what’s missing. The two tools below take fairly different approaches to that job: one is centered on AI-assisted drafting and the other on real-time optimization scoring. content Hub is HubSpot’s content management and creation platform, built to handle everything from blog posts and landing pages to podcasts, video, and other content formats within a single workspace. Breeze is the AI assistant built into that platform, designed to sit alongside the writing process rather than replace it. Instead of generating a finished draft on its own, Breeze works inside the same editor a marketer is already writing in, helping with tasks like drafting sections, suggesting edits, summarizing research, or repurposing existing content into new formats. Rather than writing a draft in one tool, running it through a separate seo checker, and copying recommendations back over, Content Hub surfaces SEO guidance directly inside the editor as the content is being written. That includes recommendations tied to keyword usage, internal linking opportunities, and structural elements like headers and meta descriptions, all visible in the same window as the draft itself. Because content Hub sits on the same platform as HubSpot’s Smart CRM and reporting tools, published content also connects automatically to the same performance data used elsewhere in the platform. It’s possible to see not just how a page is optimized at the point of publishing, but how it actually performs against leads and revenue afterward. This matters more than it might initially seem, given how much content is now produced with AI assistance in the process. The risk isn’t using AI to write; it’s using AI to write without enough human input to keep the result accurate and credible. Content Hub and Breeze are built around keeping a human in that loop by design, since the AI assists within an editor a person is actively working in, rather than producing a complete draft that gets copied in afterward with minimal review. What I like: I love how content Hub and Breeze connect humans and AI in a way that’s actually efficient. It’s a mistake to churn out high volumes of AI-written blog content without real human input. What Content Hub and Breeze allow for is AI-assisted drafting while a human reviews and writes alongside it, rather than handing the whole process over. AI acts more like a co-pilot than a content writer in this setup, and that’s so much better for brand narrative, accuracy, and the expertise that only a human can add. Surferseo is a content optimization tool that analyzes top-ranking pages for a given keyword and turns that analysis into specific, actionable recommendations inside a live editor.

Pricing: Plans start at $99/month

What I like: Surferseo makes SEO accessible for beginners or writers who don’t necessarily have the skills to analyze SERPs themselves. It bridges the gap between SEO and good writing, and I love how it pulls recommendations into a live, editable document so writers can see exactly what to add as they go. That said, while Surferseo is an excellent tool, it works best with a savvy writer who’s comfortable going against a recommendation when following it would hurt article quality. Not every recommendation needs to be applied; it’s about balance, not blindly hitting a score. Technical seo problems are often invisible to the naked eye, which is exactly why dedicated tools exist to surface crawl errors, speed issues, and structural problems before they quietly drag down rankings. The tools below range from fully integrated platform features to specialist crawlers built for deeper, hands-on audits. HubSpot’s seo recommendations tool generates recommendations by scanning an entire domain or individual pages and analyzing on-page SEO factors that affect visibility in search results. The tool surfaces issues grouped by category and impact, things like missing metadata, broken links, and missed internal linking opportunities, rather than a flat, unsorted list of problems. What makes this particularly useful for teams without a dedicated technical resource is how the recommendations can be filtered. Inside the seo tool, results can be sorted by category, impact, technical difficulty, and role, with most recommendations assigned to either the marketer or developer role. Recommendations tagged for marketers are generally things a marketer can resolve directly, like updating a meta description or fixing a heading structure. Recommendations tagged for developers usually require more hands-on technical work. However, HubSpot notes that even marketer-tagged fixes may require a developer’s help depending on how a site is built. Recommendations can also be reviewed at the individual page level inside the content editor itself, where each one shows as a green checkmark once resolved or a gray checkmark while still outstanding, making it easy to track progress page by page rather than only at the domain level. For teams that need to share findings outside the platform, recommendations can also be exported based on the filters that are currently applied. What I like: What I like most about this tool is how it prioritizes actions and assigns them to a role, either marketer or developer, rather than just dumping a long list of technical issues with no sense of who should actually be the one fixing them. This functionality is a great support to non-technical seo teams, since it removes the guesswork around what needs fixing, in what order, and who to go to for support. Instead of a marketer staring at a vague “fix this” recommendation and not knowing whether it’s something they can handle themselves, the tool tells them whether it’s in their lane or whether it’s time to loop in a developer. Google Lighthouse is a free, open-source auditing tool built into Chrome DevTools that evaluates a page across performance, accessibility, seo, and best practices. It generates a score for each category along with specific recommendations for improvement. What I like: Google Lighthouse is already built into Chrome, so there’s no setup or account creation involved. A quick audit is just a few clicks away inside the browser a marketer is already using. It’s a useful first stop for catching obvious performance and accessibility issues before reaching for a more specialized tool. Google PageSpeed Insights is a free tool that analyzes the loading performance of a specific URL, drawing on both lab data and real-world user data from Google’s Chrome User Experience Report. What I like: What stands out to me about PageSpeed Insights is the field data. Lighthouse gives a useful lab-based snapshot, but PageSpeed Insights backs that up with how the page actually performs for real visitors, which is a more reliable picture when deciding what to prioritize fixing first. Screaming Frog is a desktop-based seo crawler that scans a website much like a search engine would, surfacing technical issues like broken links, duplicate content, redirect chains, and missing metadata across an entire site at once.

Pricing: Free for crawls up to 500 URLs. Plans start at $279/year

What I like: Screaming Frog is an excellent tool, though it requires some skill to use it well, so it’s better suited to intermediate or advanced seo teams rather than complete beginners.

My two favorite things about Screaming Frog are the visualizations, which let me see exactly how a website is mapped out, and the API integrations. I connect it to GSC and GA4 for every client I work with, pulling all the data together to see how a page actually performs. Sometimes a page has low clicks but strong page visits driven by other marketing efforts, and Screaming Frog helps catch that by making it possible to view a site holistically rather than through one metric in isolation.

Rank tracking tools span a wide range, from enterprise platforms built to monitor thousands of keywords across markets to lightweight trackers built for a single site on a tight budget. The two below sit at opposite ends of that spectrum, useful for very different team sizes and needs. Ahrefs Rank Tracker is part of Ahrefs’ broader seo platform, monitoring keyword positions by device, location, and search engine, while storing historical ranking data so movement can be tracked over time and tied back to specific content updates or algorithm changes.

Pricing: Rank Tracker is included in plans starting at $129/month

What I like: What I like about Ahrefs Rank Tracker is how much context it gives beyond a simple position number. Seeing the exact date a page jumped or dropped, alongside Share of Voice and competitor comparisons, makes it far easier to connect ranking changes to specific causes, whether that’s a content update, a link building push, or a Google algorithm change. It turns rank tracking into something that actually explains movement, not just reports it.

SERPRobot is a lightweight, budget-friendly rank tracker focused on a single job: checking where a site ranks for specific keywords, without the broader suite of features bundled into larger platforms.

Pricing: Plans start at $4.99/month

Scaling an seo program past a certain size usually means outgrowing many tools — like free SEO tools — altogether. Instead, enterprise companies need platforms built to handle large content libraries, multiple markets, and reporting that stands up to enterprise scrutiny. Marketing Hub is HubSpot’s marketing platform. Paired with HubSpot AEO at the Professional and Enterprise levels, Marketing Hub becomes a dedicated AEO product that gives growing teams a single system that handles traditional seo performance and AI search visibility together, rather than treating them as separate disciplines requiring separate tools. On the seo side, Marketing Hub already brings together content creation, SEO recommendations, topic cluster planning, and CRM-connected reporting in one platform, which becomes increasingly valuable as a content library and team grow. Instead of stitching together a keyword tool, a separate technical auditor, a third reporting layer, and a CRM that doesn’t talk to any of them, everything scales inside the same system, with the same underlying data. AEO extends that same logic into AI search. HubSpot AEO tracks how a brand is represented across AI platforms like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. It includes a brand visibility score, sentiment analysis, competitor share of voice, and citation analysis showing which sources AI systems are pulling from when they generate an answer.

What sets HubSpot AEO apart from a standalone AI visibility tracker is that it’s built on the same CRM data that already powers the rest of Marketing Hub, so prompt suggestions and recommendations are based on what the platform already knows about a business and its buyers, rather than generic guesses.

When the tool identifies a visibility gap, the recommended fix can be acted on directly inside the platform, whether that means creating new content, updating an existing page, or publishing a social post. For a team that’s scaling content production and needs both search and AI visibility to scale with it, having that loop, tracking, recommendation, and execution inside one platform removes a layer of friction that becomes more costly the bigger the program gets. What I like: What I like most about Marketing Hub with AEO is that scaling doesn’t mean adding more tools; it means the same platform doing more. As a content library grows from dozens of pages to hundreds, the reporting doesn’t get messier or harder to maintain, because everything was built to connect from the start. Having AEO sit inside that same ecosystem means AI visibility is treated as part of the core strategy rather than an afterthought. BrightEdge is an enterprise seo and AI search platform built specifically for large organizations managing complex content operations across multiple markets, languages, and brands.

Pricing: Custom enterprise pricing based on domains, keyword volume, and selected modules

What I like: What I like about BrightEdge is that it’s built for actual scale instead of scaled-up versions of mid-market features. It’s the kind of platform that makes sense once a business is managing thousands of pages across multiple markets and needs both traditional rankings and AI citation tracking handled by the same system. It’s not the right fit for a small team, but for an enterprise with the budget and complexity to match, the depth of historical data and AI visibility tracking together is hard to find elsewhere. Teams that are starting out don’t need a full paid stack to make meaningful progress. The free seo optimization tools already covered in this guide are enough to audit a site, find keywords, and start fixing issues right away.

Ranking well on Google no longer guarantees visibility, since a growing share of searches are now answered directly inside AI tools without a click to any website. The tools below help measure where a brand currently stands in AI-generated answers and track whether that visibility is improving over time. HubSpot AEO Grader is a free diagnostic tool that reveals how a brand is currently represented inside AI-generated answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. After entering a brand name, location, industry, and product details, the tool runs an assessment across these platforms and returns a composite visibility score out of 100, broken down across five scored dimensions: sentiment, presence quality, brand recognition, share of voice, and market position. Each dimension comes with a written interpretation rather than just a number, making it easier to understand what’s actually driving the score. A low presence quality score, for example, points to limited third-party coverage or thin website content, while a low sentiment score signals negative associations worth investigating further. The tool is designed as a one-time benchmark rather than ongoing tracking, giving a snapshot of current AI visibility that can be rerun periodically, since answer engines update their training data regularly and brand perception can shift without warning. What I like: It’s so easy to use, completely free, and a genuine trove of information for any website. Searches can be run as many times as needed at no cost, so I use it for competitors too, not just my own clients’ brands. It’s excellent for getting an initial AEO benchmark and makes a great starting point before deciding whether ongoing tracking is worth investing in. HubSpot AEO is a dedicated tool for AI visibility tracking, picking up where a one-time AEO Grader check leaves off. Rather than a single snapshot, it continuously tracks a brand across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. It monitors a set of prompts relevant to the business and reports on visibility score, sentiment, competitor share of voice, and citation analysis that shows exactly which sources AI systems are pulling from when generating an answer. What sets HubSpot AEO apart from a standalone AI visibility tracker is its connection to HubSpot’s Smart CRM. Prompt suggestions are based on what the platform already knows about a business and its buyers, rather than generic guesses about what someone might ask. When the tool flags a visibility gap, that recommendation can lead directly to action inside HubSpot, whether that’s creating new content, updating an existing page, or publishing a social post, without needing to switch to a separate platform to follow through. HubSpot AEO is included in Marketing Hub Professional and Enterprise editions and is part of the same connected ecosystem already powering seo recommendations and reporting. AI visibility becomes one more metric tied to the same CRM and revenue data rather than a separate, disconnected dashboard.

For teams not yet on Marketing Hub Professional or Enterprise, HubSpot AEO is also available as a standalone product starting at $50/month, with a free 28-day trial that includes 25 prompts tracked across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity — no credit card required. That makes it accessible as an entry point for teams that want ongoing AI visibility tracking without committing to the full Marketing Hub ecosystem first.

AirOps is a full-stack AI search platform built to close the loop between spotting an AI visibility gap and actually fixing it, rather than stopping at a dashboard.

Pricing: Pricing starts at $200/month

What I like: AirOps is an excellent tool for marketing teams who want to stay ahead of the AI search curve. It lets them analyze content visibility and share of voice, so they can work toward getting their content cited and summarized by AI engines rather than left out of the conversation entirely. Marketing teams increasingly need a tool like this in their stack, since it’s one of the few that hands back some narrative control over how a brand actually gets represented in AI-generated answers. It depends on team size and how connected the work needs to be. Smaller teams or those just starting often do fine stitching together a few free tools, since each one tends to specialize in a single job like keyword research or technical audits. As an seo program grows, though, fragmented tools start to create fragmented reporting, making it harder to prove ROI. This is where consolidated SEO platforms become more useful, since they cover multiple jobs in one place and keep performance data connected rather than scattered across logins. ROI from seo tools is best measured by tracing organic traffic through to leads, conversions, and revenue, not just traffic or ranking improvements on their own. A page might rank well and get plenty of visits without generating any business value, so the more useful question is what happens after someone lands on it. seo tools that connect to a CRM make measuring ROI far easier, since they can show which organic content actually contributed to a deal closing, rather than leaving traffic and revenue as two separate, disconnected numbers. A handful of free seo tools are usually enough to get started, since free SEO tools are useful for quick audits, research, and early-stage testing before committing budget to anything paid. Tools like HubSpot’s Website Grader, Google Search Console, and Google Lighthouse cover audits and technical checks, while Google Analytics adds visibility into how content performs more broadly. None of these require a subscription, making it possible to build real momentum before paid tools become necessary. The best tools for seo connect to a CRM through native integrations or APIs, pulling organic traffic and ranking data into the same system that tracks leads and deals. Others, like HubSpot’s Marketing Hub, are built on top of the CRM directly, so SEO performance and customer data live in the same platform from the start rather than being joined together after the fact. This kind of integration is what makes it possible to see which organic content is actually influencing pipeline and revenue, not just traffic. A full technical audit is generally worth running every one to three months, though this can vary depending on how often a site changes. Sites with frequent content updates, redesigns, or migrations benefit from more regular checks, since technical issues tend to surface after changes rather than during quiet periods. Lighter, more frequent checks, like a quick Lighthouse or PageSpeed scan, can run more often in between full audits to catch obvious issues early. The right tool stack isn’t about owning every single tool. Instead, try tools that cover the five core jobs: keyword research, technical audits, rank tracking, on-page optimization, and reporting, without paying for more overlap than necessary. If you want to start risk-free, then free tools are the way forward. Try Website Grader, AEO Grader, Google Search Console, and Lighthouse. Once you start with free tools, you might find extensions of pro packages are worth it. For example, I use Marketing Hub in my own business, and I recently upgraded my plan because I needed an email feature. Once I upgraded, I realized how much more I unlocked from the tool. I could suddenly see seo, email, and overall site performance sitting in the same place instead of pieced together across separate tools. That’s really the pattern with tools like HubSpot: the upgrade rarely benefits one feature.
