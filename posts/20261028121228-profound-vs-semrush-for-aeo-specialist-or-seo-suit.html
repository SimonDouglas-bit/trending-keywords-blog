One more note on signals. Visibility in AI answers, citations, referral traffic, and deals are four different measurements. Improving one doesn’t prove causal revenue impact, and no tool here offers complete revenue attribution.

AEO, or answer engine optimization, is the practice of earning accurate, favorable mentions and citations in AI-generated answers. SEO targets visibility in traditional results. The two share plenty of tactics, though they differ in measurement, tooling, and what winning means. Many teams treat AEO as part of a broader generative engine optimization strategy.

Profound is a specialist AEO platform. It was built for answer engine optimization, server-log crawler analytics, SKU-level AI shopping data, and no-code content automation. Semrush offers AEO visibility features inside a broader SEO suite, tying AI visibility to its keyword database, technical site audits, and backlink profiles.

The shift is worth acting on. Pew Research Center found that users clicked a traditional result link in 8% of visits when a Google AI summary appeared, versus 15% of visits without one. Gartner predicted that traditional search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026 as AI chatbots and virtual agents take over queries, though that figure is a forecast, not a measured result.

AEO tool selection depends on engine coverage, prompt intelligence, crawler analytics, workflows, pricing, and team fit. Engine coverage is the easiest place to start because the numbers are concrete, and a tool that tracks only one engine leaves you blind to the others.

Profound tiers its coverage. Starter tracks ChatGPT only, Growth tracks three engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews), and Enterprise expands to up to 10 engines, including Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Meta AI, Grok, and DeepSeek. It captures answers through browser-based runs that mirror what consumer-facing interfaces show, on a daily cadence. In our judgment, Growth is the realistic starting point for a serious program, since Starter leaves gaps on Perplexity and Google AI Overviews.

Semrush starts broader. Its base plans monitor five engines: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Google AI Mode. Enterprise plans add models like Grok and Claude.

Skip the raw engine count and ask three questions:

Prompt research supports content planning the way keyword research does in traditional SEO, and the quality of your prompt list sets the quality of your visibility data. The two tools take different approaches.

Profound uses double opt-in consumer panel data to discover AI queries, classify buyer intent (informational, commercial, conversational, or generative), and break down prompt volume by age, income bracket, and region. For each tracked prompt, you can review the captured answer, which sits closer to what a buyer sees than sampled data would.

Semrush draws on a database of more than 239 million LLM prompts, backed by its broader dataset of 26.8 billion keywords and 808 million domain profiles. That helps teams starting from zero find prompts already driving answers in their category. Both tools let you track custom prompts.

Neither source is universally better. Panel data shows how real people phrase questions, which helps with demographic targeting. A search-derived index scales across categories and connects to the keyword work your team already does. Manually curated prompts give you control, but they reflect your assumptions about buyers, so validate them against one of the other two sources.

Pro tip: Before you build content around a prompt, run it manually in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. If a tool shows you as absent but you appear in manual checks, investigate the tracking gap before you spend content budget.

This is Profound’s sharpest advantage. Its Agent Analytics reads server logs from providers like Cloudflare, Vercel, Akamai, AWS, and Fastly to track how often AI crawlers visit, verify real AI bots versus spoofed ones, measure human referral traffic from AI, and submit new content to AI crawlers. For ecommerce, its ChatGPT Shopping module tracks product recommendations, attribute accuracy, and shopper sentiment at the SKU level.

Semrush takes a site-audit approach. Its AI-Ready Site Audit checks site health for AI crawlers and flags technical blockages and errors. Ecommerce tracking runs through its broader Traffic and Market Insights and SERP tools, so it’s useful context without being SKU-level.

Server logs show what bots requested, and a site audit shows what could block them, so the two answer different questions. Neither means a page will be included or cited, because access is a precondition — not a guarantee. If you’re building the measurement side from scratch, this overview of AI web analytics explains how AI-driven visits show up in your reporting.

Pro tip: Before you buy a tool, check your server logs for AI bot traffic. Seeing which pages GPTBot or PerplexityBot already request tells you where to start.

Measurement tells you where you stand, and workflow tells you what to do about it. Profound and Semrush split here.

Profound tracks mentions, share of voice, sentiment (positive, neutral, or negative), and citation domains. Profound Agents is a no-code, drag-and-drop workflow builder, and Profound Sheets is a spreadsheet environment where each column acts as an automated agent that generates AEO briefs, FAQs, and content. It includes human-in-the-loop approvals and direct publishing to CMSs like WordPress, Contentful, Framer, and Sanity. Higher tiers add CSV and JSON exports, API access, and SSO/SAML.

Semrush tracks brand visibility, AI share of voice, sentiment, audience query intent, and narrative drivers (branded versus non-branded citations). Its Content Toolkit generates SEO and AEO briefs, aligns brand voice, and handles multichannel publishing. An MCP server lets you query live Semrush data conversationally inside Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, or VS Code, and add-on toolkits cover local SEO, social, advertising, and AI PR, which targets media outlets cited by LLMs. Reporting extends through Looker Studio integration on SEO Pro+ and API access on SEO Advanced.

Semrush’s strength is that AEO data sits beside rank tracking and backlink audits, so one report can cover classic and AI visibility, including paid work like search engine marketing if your team runs it. Profound’s strength is how far its automation reaches into content production. Whichever you choose, a dashboard alone changes nothing until someone acts on it.

Semrush (AI Visibility on its own, or bundled in Semrush One):

HubSpot AEO costs $50 per month ($45 if you pay annually), with 25 prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity included and more available to buy. The free trial covers the same 25 prompts and needs no credit card.

Entry price tells you very little. Work through these steps:

Pick Profound if you’re an AI-first marketing team or ecommerce brand that needs SKU-level shopping insights, server-log verification of AI bots, demographic prompt research, or no-code content automation. Pick Semrush if you want AI visibility bundled with rank tracking, technical audits, backlink analysis, local SEO, and social management in one subscription.

Semrush’s AI Visibility plan or a lower Semrush One tier fits best if you also need SEO tools, since one subscription covers keywords and five engines from day one. Profound’s Starter costs the same $99 but covers one engine. HubSpot AEO is the lower-cost start if you’d rather begin with fewer features.

Pro tip: Start with 25 to 50 prompts that map to your highest-intent buyer questions. A small, clean prompt set beats a sprawling one you never review.

Profound is the stronger fit here, particularly for ecommerce teams and organizations that need compliance documentation. Its server-log analytics and SKU-level shopping data have no Semrush equivalent, and Enterprise adds SOC 2 Type II compliance and SSO. Regulated teams should also build human review into any AI-generated content and check privacy terms on panel and log data.

Pro tip: If your team already lives in Semrush for SEO, run a short side-by-side on 50 shared prompts before committing to a second tool.

Semrush suits agencies managing many domains under one roof, since its plans scale by website count (5, 15, then 40). Profound fits when clients need log-level proof or when its workflows can standardize deliverables. HubSpot AEO also lets you buy one instance per brand at $50 per month.

Pro tip: Price per client, not per account. Domain and seat limits change the math quickly.

HubSpot AEO tracks brand visibility across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. It’s currently in beta, and it covers the loop most teams care about: a brand visibility score, prompt tracking, citation analysis, competitor share of voice, and prioritized recommendations for what to create, update, or fix. The price is $50 per month ($45 if you pay annually) and includes 25 prompts, with more available to buy. You can try it free for 25 prompts with no credit card, and you don’t need to host your site on HubSpot’s CMS.

AEO in Marketing Hub is a related option. It’s included with Marketing Hub Pro and Enterprise, uses CRM data to inform prompt suggestions, and lets you act on recommendations inside HubSpot’s content and social tools. CRM data informs which prompts to suggest. It doesn’t reproduce the literal questions your customers ask, so review suggested prompts like you would any other.

The tradeoffs are clear, and worth stating up front: HubSpot AEO covers three engines, where Semrush covers five on base plans and Profound reaches 10 on Enterprise. It also lacks server-log crawler analytics, a SKU-level shopping module, and backlink data. In return you get the lowest entry price in this comparison, a short setup, and recommendations that sit next to the content, contacts, and campaigns your team already manages. For inbound teams already working in HubSpot, that connection can matter more than extra engines.

Tools measure, and execution moves the numbers — so the habits below matter more than the logo on your dashboard. These practices apply no matter which tool you choose.

Researchers behind the GEO paper (Princeton and collaborators, published at KDD 2024) found that tactics like adding citations, quotations from relevant sources, and statistics can boost a source’s visibility in generative engine responses by up to 40% in their tests. That’s a ceiling from a research benchmark, not a promise, and results vary by domain. The practical takeaway still holds:

Add AI answers to the reports you already run. Weekly, track brand visibility score by engine, share of voice against your top competitors, citation rate by content type, and traffic from AI referral sources.

Some AI referrals arrive without referrer data, so they can land in direct traffic. Create a dedicated segment for known AI sources such as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, and gemini.google.com, then connect those sessions to contacts and deals in your CRM. You’ll see trackable referral traffic and deals, which is useful, though it isn’t complete revenue attribution, because many buyers read an AI answer and visit later through another channel.

Switch when the suite’s AEO layer blocks a decision you need to make. Common triggers are needing server-log verification of AI bots, SKU-level shopping analytics, demographic prompt research, or more engines than your plan covers. If you’re still learning which prompts matter, a suite layer is usually enough.

Check where your buyers research. Ask sales and customers which tools they use, review your AI referral traffic by source, and compare two or three engines on the same prompt set. If the answers and citations look similar, extra engines add little. If they diverge, broader coverage earns its price.

Profound is the right call for specialist needs: log analytics, SKU-level shopping data, and workflow automation at scale. Semrush is the right call when AEO should live beside the SEO tools you already use. If your team works in HubSpot and wants ongoing visibility tracking next to your content and CRM, HubSpot AEO is worth a trial before you add anything heavier.
