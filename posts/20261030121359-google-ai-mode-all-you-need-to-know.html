Google announced AI Mode as a Labs experiment on 5 March 2025. Two months later, it was open to everyone in the US. By October 2025, it was in more than 200 countries and territories.

Then, at Google I/O in May 2026, Sundar Pichai called AI Mode "our biggest upgrade to Search ever" and said it had passed 1 billion monthly active users.

It's no longer just an experiment. AI Mode now has its own report in Search Console, its own opt-out setting, its own shopping cart, and agents that work in the background.

Here's everything SEOs and content marketers need to know about it.

Explore our index of 101 million AI Mode prompts in Ahrefs’ Brand Radar.

AI Mode is Google's conversational search experience. You ask a question, and instead of a list of links, you get an AI-generated answer with links to the sources it used. Then you can keep asking follow-up questions.

In classic Google search, you get a list of links and have to click through one or more of them. If you don't find what you need, you search again. In AI Mode, the searching happens behind the scenes, and you get the answer directly.

At I/O 2026, Google made Gemini 3.5 Flash the default model for AI Mode globally.

Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also pick Gemini 4 Argon, Google's most capable model, for harder multi-step questions.

However, this can change whenever Google releases new models.

AI Mode isn't just a chat box anymore. Here's what it can do:

Search with any input: Type, speak, upload images and files, paste a URL to ask about a page, or add Chrome tabs.

Search Live: Have a voice and camera conversation with Search in the Google app. Available everywhere AI Mode is.

Generative UI: AI Mode can build interactive visuals, charts, calculators, and simulations on the fly. Launched globally in English in August 2026. To get one, ask for it ("show me an interactive simulation of…").

Info monitoring: Ask AI Mode to keep you updated on a topic, and it watches the web in the background and sends you updates.

Agentic booking: AI Mode can find restaurant reservations, event tickets, local appointments, and experiences, and link you to book them. For some categories, like home repair, beauty, and pet care, it can call businesses on your behalf (US).

Shopping: Conversational shopping powered by Google's Shopping Graph (60B+ product listings), agentic checkout with Google Pay at eligible US merchants, and AI that calls local stores to check stock and prices.

Universal Cart: One cart for products from different retailers, across Search and the Gemini app. It tracks price drops and flags deals. Launched in the US on 15 October 2026.

Connected apps: Link Instacart, Canva, or YouTube Music and act on them inside AI Mode (US).

Notebooks: Organize sources and past AI Mode threads in a notebook inside Search.

The agentic features are the ones to look out for. When an agent books the table or adds the product to a cart, the user may never visit your website at all.

AI Overviews are the AI summaries that appear at the top of regular search results. AI Mode is a separate, conversational experience.

Google says the two "may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary."

In fact, when we compared 730,000 AI Mode and AI Overview responses for the same queries, only 13.7% of the URLs they cite overlap. The answers mean pretty much the same thing (86% semantic similarity), but they're written differently and cite different pages.

In other words, getting cited in AI Overviews doesn't mean you'll be cited in AI Mode.

At I/O 2026, Google merged the two into "one, seamless AI Search experience". You can now go from a regular search, to an AI Overview, to a follow-up question in AI Mode, without ever leaving the results page.

In classic search, Google takes your query and ranks pages for it.

AI Mode uses a technique called query fan-out. In Google's words, it divides "your question into subtopics and [searches] for each one simultaneously across multiple data sources." It then combines the results into one answer.

For example, a prompt like "Could you suggest Bluetooth headphones with a comfortable over-ear design and long-lasting battery?" could fan out into:

Best Bluetooth headphones with over-ear design

Top-rated over-ear Bluetooth headphones with long battery

Most comfortable over-ear Bluetooth headphones

Bluetooth headphones with the longest battery life

Which Bluetooth over-ear headphones charge fastest?

How many searches does it run? Research by Seer Interactive and Nectiv found an average of 9–11 fan-out queries per prompt, with some prompts triggering up to 28.

And more than 95% of fan-out queries have no measurable search volume. You can't "keyword research" your way into every one of them.

Once AI Mode has the results, it pulls the relevant parts (paragraphs, tables, images) from each source and writes one answer. It then grounds the answer by linking claims to the pages they came from.

Google says these features rely on "our core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages." So the pages AI Mode can draw from are pages Google can find and rank.

It appears that Google treats every follow-up question as a new search.

At least according to Google's Search Console documentation: "if a user asks a follow-up question within AI Mode, they are essentially performing a new query. All impression, position, and click data in the new response are counted as coming from this new user query."

AI Mode can also be personal. According to Google, Personal Intelligence "references previous searches and activity saved in your Search Services History" and "can learn from your past interactions" to tailor responses. You can also connect Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Photos so answers take your own context into account.

So two signed-in people asking the same question can get different answers. Even the same person can get a different answer on a different day.

When we ran a study on 43,000 AI Overview keywords, we found that AI Overviews change their wording about every two days, and 45.5% of cited URLs change between updates. However, the meaning stays consistent.

Expect AI Mode to behave similarly.

When I first wrote this post, nobody really knew. Now we have Google's own explanation and some solid data.

According to Google, AI Mode relies on "our core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from our Search index." It then reviews those pages and links the ones that support its answer.

To be eligible at all, your page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet.

Since query fan-out widens the pools, there is some research showing that ranking for the original query matters less than you'd think. A Moz study of ~40,000 queries found only 1 in 10 AI Mode citations matched a URL in Google's top 10 for the same query.

But overall search visibility still counts: SE Ranking found a site's Google traffic was the strongest predictor of how often AI Mode cited it.

AI Mode also prefers citing UGC content. If we look at the top 10 most cited domains in AI Mode, Reddit, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Quora, and TikTok make up more than half:

Finally, from May 2026, users can now add Preferred Sources to AI Mode. Links from those sites get a "Preferred" label, and people are twice as likely to click them. If you have a loyal audience, ask them to add you.

Our study showed that the presence of an AI Overview reduced clicks to top-ranking content by 58%.

We expect this to be worse with AI Mode. Similarweb found AI Mode sends visitors to websites on only 1.6–2.5% of queries, compared with 17–19% for traditional Google search.

It makes sense. In AI Mode, users can keep asking questions without clicking anything.

Google argues that the clicks AI features do send are "higher quality." I’ll leave it up to you to decide if you want to believe Google.

Google originally lumped AI Mode in with regular search and gave you no way to see it separately. Fortunately, that’s changed.

Since 31 August 2026, every site has a Generative AI performance report in Search Console. It shows:

Impressions only. An impression is counted each time a link to your site is shown to someone in an AI Overview or AI Mode answer, whether they click it or not. The report doesn't show clicks, CTR, or the queries that triggered those impressions.

AI Overviews and AI Mode combined. You can't separate them.

Breakdowns by page, country, date, and device, plus a filter for text vs. image searches (Lens, Circle to Search).

One thing to watch out for: these impressions aren't extra. Google still counts them in your main Performance report too, mixed in with your regular search results under the "Web" search type. The new report just shows you which part of that total came from AI features.

For example, say your main report shows 100,000 impressions and the generative AI report shows 20,000. You had 100,000 impressions in total, and 20,000 of them came from AI Overviews or AI Mode. You did not have 120,000.

Search Console only tells you how often your pages showed up in AI Overviews and AI Mode combined. Google published official guidance on generative AI search. Tl;dr:

To appear in AI Mode, your page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet.

"AEO" and "GEO" are still SEO. Google says the same best practices apply.

Create "non-commodity" content. Commodity content repeats common knowledge that anyone could write, like "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers." Non-commodity content adds something only you can, such as first-hand experience or original data. Google's example is "Why We Waived the Inspection & Saved Money: A Look Inside the Sewer Line."

Don't create pages for every fan-out variation. Google says mass-producing these can violate its scaled content abuse policy.

Don't bother with llms.txt, "chunking" content for AI, special AI schema, or artificial brand mentions like paying to appear on listicles.

If AI Mode is citing outdated third-party pages, update your profiles on sites like YouTube, G2, and Google Business Profile.

Citations are one thing. Being mentioned in the answer is another. We studied 75,000 brands across ChatGPT, AI Mode, and AI Overviews to see which factors correlate with how often a brand is mentioned in AI responses. AI Mode showed the strongest link to brand signals of the three.

You can build mentions by:

Getting featured in "best of" lists and industry roundups

Encouraging customers to mention you in reviews and case studies

Creating original data and research that people cite

Running digital PR campaigns

Building a product people talk about on their own

AI Mode can summarize anything that's common knowledge. It's already replacing a lot of the reference content people used to click through to. If your page says what ten other pages say, AI Mode can answer without you.

Google's advice is to create "non-commodity" content — "unique, expert-led content that provides value beyond common knowledge." That means things AI can't make up on its own:

Original research and data, e.g., Freddie Mac's weekly mortgage rate survey, the J.D. Power Vehicle Dependability Study, and The Knot's average wedding cost study. J.D. Power's ISP and rental car study pages get almost no organic traffic, but AI Mode still cites them, because nobody else has that data.

Free tools and calculators, e.g., Matic's home insurance calculator, Zillow's affordability calculator, and Crawlpedia's tire size converter.

First-hand experience and opinions, e.g., Consumer Reports' lab-tested washing machines, PCMag's photo printing services roundup, and Edmunds' midsize three-row SUV comparison test.

AI Mode can only cite pages that are already in Google's index.

Google says a page just needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in regular Search. So there's nothing new to set up. You just need to make sure nothing is stopping Google from crawling, rendering, and reading your pages:

Make sure Googlebot can crawl your pages, both in robots.txt and in your CDN or firewall settings.

Put important content in text, not just images or video.

Follow JavaScript SEO best practices so Google can render your pages.

If you're a retailer or local business, your website isn't the only thing AI Mode looks at. Its shopping and local answers also pull from structured data feeds.

Google recommends keeping your Merchant Center feed and Google Business Profile accurate and complete. Retailers can also enable Business Agent, a branded chatbot in Search, and check how they appear in AI Mode with Merchant Center's AI performance insights.

To my surprise, we’re not there yet.

AI Mode is still a separate option. But Google has made it effortless to slide into AI Mode from any search, put it at the center of I/O, and given it agents that act for you.

My take hasn't changed. I expect search to just answer any query. The genie isn't going back in the bottle.

Search Console has a Search generative AI control under Settings. It has three options:

Include (the default): your content can appear in and help ground AI answers

Exclude: your content won't appear in AI Overviews, AI Mode, or Discover's AI features, and you won't get any traffic or impressions from them

Inherit from parent: follow the setting of a parent property

A few things to know:

It works per Search Console property. You can exclude a section (like /blog/) by setting it on a URL-prefix property, but you can't exclude individual pages yet.
