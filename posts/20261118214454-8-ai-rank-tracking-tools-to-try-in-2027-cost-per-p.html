AI rank tracking tools help you see how often ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and other AI platforms recommend your brand, where you appear in their answers, and how you compare with competitors.

But not all tools track visibility the same way, and their pricing can be difficult to compare.

In this guide, I’ll compare eight AI rank tracking tools by price and features. I’ll also share findings from my own research into AI rankings, including why tracking brand mentions often tells you more than tracking positions alone.

Tools that track rankings in AI answers are usually called AI visibility tools or AI visibility trackers. Rankings are just one feature, and usually not the main one: the broader focus is brand mentions, citations, and competitor visibility.

They collect answers by scraping AI chat interfaces—sometimes through third-party data providers—or through API calls. Scraping captures the consumer-facing experience, so it can be more representative of what users see than API responses. APIs provide an alternative, including when scraping isn’t available; Brand Radar, for example, uses Claude’s API and scraping for the other 6 AI systems.

The tools then parse and store those answers, identify brands and sources, and calculate metrics such as mention rate and position.

In Ahrefs Brand Radar, for example, you can see the order in which tracked brands were mentioned in a single answer—their ordinal rank:

This shows which tracked brand was named #1, #2, and so on. Being mentioned first doesn’t necessarily mean being recommended as the best.

There’s more nuance to interpreting rankings in AEO, which we’ll get to later.

A good way to find the right AI visibility tool is to test two or three using the same 10 prompts and compare the results.

Some tools offer cheaper annual billing or lower-tier plans with different tracking limits. Costs may also vary by platform and checking frequency.

Higher-volume plans can bring the cost down considerably. For example, Ahrefs Brand Radar's Scale plan works out to $0.30 per daily tracked prompt, while SE Visible's Agency plan costs about $0.40.

Price is a useful starting point, but it doesn't tell the whole story. Let's take a closer look at what each tool includes, where it stands out, and what limitations to consider.

With custom prompt tracking, you pay per check: one prompt run on one AI platform in one location. You choose what to monitor and whether to check it daily, weekly, or monthly. Basic costs $50/month for 2,500 checks, Growth $100 for 7,000, and Scale $250 for 25,000. That's $20, about $14 and $10 per 1,000 answers, respectively, on platforms that use one check per answer. Claude uses eight checks per answer.

Extra checks cost $0.010 to $0.020 each if you enable pay-as-you-go; otherwise, tracking pauses when your allowance runs out. You don't need an Ahrefs subscription to track custom prompts.

The separate AI Visibility Index lets you explore existing AI answers without setting up prompts. It covers more than 500 million questions, with data dating back to 2025, and estimates impressions based on how frequently people search for those topics. Access starts at $199/month per platform or $699/month for all platforms, including 2,500 custom-prompt checks. The index updates monthly.

Traditional SEO focuses heavily on where a page ranks. AI search is different because the order of recommendations can change even when the same brands keep appearing.

To understand how much rankings fluctuate, I tracked 15 "best tools" prompts across seven AI platforms for 76 days, collecting 6,790 answers that mentioned at least three tools.

The results showed just how unstable individual positions can be.

When a tool appeared on consecutive days, it kept the same position only 36% of the time.

Rankings changed on 77% of days in Gemini, compared with 53% in Google AI Overviews, the most stable platform in the study.

But the brands AI chose to recommend were much more consistent.

Most prompts had around three tools that appeared in nearly every answer. When these regularly recommended tools appeared, they were mentioned again the following day 98% of the time, even though their positions often changed.

Think of it as a guest list that stays largely the same, while everyone switches seats.

My research also found a strong relationship between how often a brand gets mentioned and how high it appears.

Tools mentioned most frequently appeared in the top three positions in 61% of answers that named them. For tools mentioned only occasionally, that figure was just 12%.

This suggests that both metrics reflect how strongly AI associates a brand with a topic.

But mention rate is usually the more useful starting point because average rankings leave out an important detail: how often your brand doesn't appear at all.

For example, in one set of prompts I tracked, Ahrefs appeared in only 30% of answers. Yet when it appeared, its average position was number one.

Looking at ranking alone would make that performance seem much stronger than it really was.

Instead of reporting a single AI ranking position, track these three metrics for each AI platform. Compare the last four weeks with the four weeks before to see what's changing.

You can find most of these metrics directly in Ahrefs Brand Radar.

For any custom calculations, you can use Agent A in Ahrefs. Just describe what you want to measure, and it can work with your data. You can also export the data or connect it to another AI assistant through an API or MCP.

For example, here's a prompt you can use to calculate mention rate and average position:

Calculate Ahrefs’ mention rate for each date when any of the tools included in the report were mentioned. Also, calculate the average position at which Ahrefs appeared. Exclude Ahrefs-branded prompts from both calculations.

Ironically, rank tracking might be the least important feature to look for in an AI rank tracker.

My research showed that positions can change frequently even when the same brands keep appearing. Knowing how often AI includes your brand tells you more than a snapshot of where it places you.

So it’s more useful to think of these as AI visibility tools. Look beyond rankings to what else they help you understand: which questions bring up your brand, which sources get cited, and how you compare with competitors.

Choose the tool that helps you decide what to investigate and improve, then price the setup you actually need: the prompts, platforms, and checking schedule. If you’re buying for clients, compare agency plans separately. That’s a stronger basis for choosing than whether it can tell you you’re number one.
