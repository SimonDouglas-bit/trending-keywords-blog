By Louise Linehan

Content Marketer at Ahrefs

Ask most SEOs how google search gets its information and they'll say it crawls the web.

That's true, but it's only part of the answer.

google search gets its information in seven main ways:

Below, I’ll show you how each one works, with proof, and what you can do (if anything) to influence it.

Back in 2020, google published its own answer to this question, listing five sources. AI features, new data deals, and a series of leaks have added to that list since, so here's the 2026 version: The biggest source of google search's information is the public web pages it crawls and stores in its index. When you search, google scours that index, which it says "covers hundreds of billions of webpages and is well over 100,000,000 gigabytes in size." google also indexes content from platforms it owns, such as YouTube, Books and Scholar.

And social media pages on Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Quora and forums get crawled just like any other page.

google's crawler, Googlebot, finds these pages by following links from pages it already knows and by reading sitemaps, then processes and stores them. Links are information in their own right, because they tell google what other sites think of a page. According to google: "the number of quality pages that link to a particular page is a signal that a page may be a trusted source of information on a topic." Documents from the US Department of Justice's (DOJ) antitrust case against google tell us more about this. The DOJ sued google in 2020 for illegally monopolizing search, and the trial made dozens of internal Google documents and testimony public. One of them, a 2017 presentation by google engineer Eric Lehman called "Life of a Click", names links as one of the "3 pillars of ranking": So google learns two things from the open web: what each page says about itself, and what the rest of the web says about it through links and anchor text. Yes, you can influence the open web in two ways: by earning links from other sites and by giving google access to your pages. If few anchors say what you actually do, google is learning little about your brand and its topic from the rest of the web.

I asked Agent A to analyze all of our brand-rich anchors and surrounding text, and found out that just 27% of our total links describe what we actually do.

And, of that group, 30% describe Ahrefs as a keyword research tool, while 6.5% refer to us an AI visibility platform. You can use this kind of data as a to-do list: update the descriptions that sites copy (your About page, bios and review profiles), and ask those sites to update their wording when they link back to you. But before you do any of that, you need to make sure google can find your pages in the first place. google only knows what it can crawl, render, and index, so if Googlebot can't do those things, your content isn't part of Google's information, however good it may be! Run a crawl in Ahrefs' Site Audit (free for your own sites with Ahrefs Webmaster Tools) and open the "Indexability" report to find out which pages are blocked or kept out of google's index, and work out why that is.

Then go ahead and fix those issues.

google also gets information directly from public knowledge bases, which is where most of the facts in Google's knowledge panels come from. google says this information comes from "openly licensed sources" like Wikipedia and Data Commons, and from structured databases like Wikidata. It even pays for some of it: in 2022, google became the first customer of Wikimedia Enterprise, a faster feed of Wikipedia content. These facts feed the Knowledge Graph, google's database of people, places, and things. By google's last official count, in 2020, it held over 500 billion facts about five billion entities. google's AI uses these sources too.

For instance, Wikipedia was cited in 72% of the branded AI Overviews we studied.

You can partly influence public knowledge bases: you can’t write your own Wikipedia entry, but you can make your facts easy to confirm.

Some of google's information is paid for or supplied by partners, rather than crawled. google says it licenses "specialized types of data, like sports scores, information about TV shows and movies, and song lyrics" from providers that deliver it in a structured format.

News is also a growing part of the mix. For example, since 2025 the Associated Press has sent a real-time news feed to the Gemini app.

And in 2024, google announced an expanded partnership with Reddit, giving it access to Reddit’s Data API, content it can “display, train on, and otherwise use.” You can influence licensed data indirectly, but not directly. google licenses content from Reddit and newswires, and you can shape what they say about your company. Some information never needs crawling, because businesses hand it to google. According to google, businesses can appear in search through their Business Profile "even if they don't have a website." That's how google gets the hours, address, and phone number in a local business listing. As well as local businesses, airlines, retailers, and manufacturers send google data about their products and inventory "through direct feeds," which is how Google can show flight details or a product's specs straight from the source. Retailers do this through Merchant Center, and those product feeds help build google's Shopping Graph, which holds over 60 billion product listings. All of this data also feeds google's AI answers. For instance, AI Mode draws on "shopping data for billions of products" to answer product questions. You can influence business information directly, which makes it one of the easiest sources for a business to shape. It's not just businesses that supply google with information. Individual searchers do too through reviews, Maps edits, and YouTube videos. google Maps alone has more than 500 million contributors, who add reviews, ratings, photos, answers to questions, and address updates every day. Waze, which google owns, adds to this: its drivers report accidents and other hazards, and Google Maps now shows those Waze reports. And then, of course, you have YouTube. Creators upload their videos, and google shows them in video results and carousels in search. They show up in google's AI answers too, where YouTube was the most-cited domain in AI Overviews among the top 50 in our November 2026 data.

You can influence what people contribute, because you can add your own content and encourage your customers to add theirs.

To get your videos into google's AI answers too, run the YouTube report in Brand Radar for your competitors and sort by the "Cited in AI" column. These are the competitor videos google's AI already cites.

Searchers give google information without realizing it, through what they search for and click. This might be google’s most valuable source. Documents from the DOJ’s antitrust trial show google software engineer Eric Lehman telling staff in a late-2016 presentation, the “Q4 Search All Hands”:

“We do not understand documents. We fake it… Beyond some basic stuff, we hardly look at documents. We look at people.”

google learns from which results people choose and which they abandon. Lehman’s “Life of a Click” deck lists user interactions as the third pillar of ranking: The system behind this is Navboost, which google Vice President of search Pandu Nayak testified uses about 13 months of click data to adjust rankings. In other words, every search and click is a data point, which makes searchers themselves one of google’s sources of information. google also watches behavior in its other products. YouTube, for example, says it may consider your search and watch history, if you have it turned on, when deciding what to show you. And google gets information from paid human raters—that is, if you define “information” as more than just facts and content. Over 10,000 search quality raters run sample searches and score the results against google’s public guidelines. According to court documents, RankEmbed, one of google’s main ranking models, is trained on search logs plus those scores, so human judgments help shape the model. So google’s information doesn’t only come from web pages: it also comes from the people searching and the people rating.

You can partly influence how people behave. You can’t control clicks, but you can improve the odds that yours is the page people choose and stay on.

Some information doesn't exist anywhere until google produces it. google's own list of sources includes "newly created information and insights," like sunrise and sunset times it calculates from a location's coordinates.

It also works out how busy places usually are (Popular Times) and builds AI flood forecasts for more than 100 countries.

The newest thing google creates is text: Gemini, the AI model behind Google's AI answers, writes new responses from what it has learned (more on this below). You can’t influence how google creates information. But when it comes to AI content, you can monitor what its AI responses say about you.

More on that below.

google's AI gets information from the same places as regular search, plus whatever it has picked up in training. Training is where the model learns from huge amounts of data. For instance, google’s AI features run on customized versions of its Gemini model, and Gemini itself learned from publicly available web documents, code, images, audio and video, and more. google’s AI models can also look things up when you ask.

That’s called grounding, or retrieval-augmented generation.

Whenever you search, google’s AI fetches live results from the SERPs (which draw on the seven sources above) and writes the answer from what it finds.

That keeps the answer current and less likely to be made up.

Nobody outside google knows how much of an answer comes from training versus retrieval.

But one clue we do have is which pages it links to or “cites”.

Citations are the one visible trace of google’s retrieval. We used Ahrefs Brand Radar to group the 50 most-cited domains in AI Overviews and AI Mode by type of source, to get a better idea of how google’s AI gets its information. google's own properties and user-generated platforms take nearly 80% of those citations, while publishers and brands get 10 to 12%. YouTube and google.com lead Google's own properties, and Reddit and Facebook lead the user-generated platforms. Publishers include sites like Healthline and NerdWallet. Most google citations point back to Google Shopping product listings, Google Finance quotes, place and hotel listings, and sports scores. You can partly influence google's AI answers by controlling whether your content can be retrieved and/or trained on. google collects information about you whenever you use its products. google's privacy policy says this includes your searches and other activity, your location from GPS or IP address, Chrome history synced to your account, and activity on sites that use Google services like Google Analytics. It uses this to personalize your results, and since January 2026, AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can opt in to Personal Intelligence, which connects Gmail and google Photos to AI Mode. In search, that personalization draws on your location, past search history, and search settings, and google can even move a page you visit repeatedly higher up your results. Your personal data shapes only your own results, though searches and clicks across millions of people feed rankings for everyone (see source 6 above).

Not always, in regular results or in AI answers.

google admits it "often can't tell from the words or images alone if something is exaggerated, incorrect, low-quality or otherwise unhelpful," which is why it relies on signals like links and behavior. For AI Overviews, google says mistakes usually come from misreading the query or a nuance of language, or from a lack of good information to draw on. To fix wrong information about you, claim your knowledge panel or Business Profile to edit it, or use the feedback link on the panel or AI Overview. There's plenty of information google can't see or won't show: google's information comes from far more than the pages it crawls, and its AI features pull from all seven sources. If you want to supply google with information, the easiest way to do that is by focusing on the sources you control: make your site easy to crawl, encourage other sites to link to you, feed Google directly through Business Profiles, product feeds, and YouTube, and build a brand people search for and choose.
